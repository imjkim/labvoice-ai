# SOP-120 Fixation
Version 3 | Effective 2026-01-15 | Owner: Histology supervisor

## 1. Purpose and scope
Defines the fixative, volume and fixation times for tissue received in histology, so that morphology and biomarker testing (IHC) are reliable.

## 2. Fixative and volume
Use 10% neutral buffered formalin. The volume of formalin must be at least ten times the volume of tissue. Large specimens are sectioned (bread-loafed) by the pathologist assistant so fixative can penetrate. Never use formalin that is past its expiry date or has been reused.

## 3. Breast specimens for ER, PR and HER2
Breast specimens that may be tested for ER, PR or HER2 must be fixed for at least 6 hours and no more than 72 hours, and the cold ischemia time should be one hour or less. If fixation falls outside 6 to 72 hours, the IHC is still performed but the LIMS case must carry a note so the pathologist can qualify the result. Never start processing a breast specimen before 6 hours of fixation.

## 4. Small biopsies
Small biopsies (cores, endoscopic and skin punch biopsies) need at least 6 hours in formalin before processing. Biopsies received late in the day are held in formalin and processed on the overnight biopsy program.

## 5. Bone and calcified tissue
Calcified specimens are fixed completely before decalcification. Use EDTA decalcification when IHC or molecular testing may be needed; strong acid decalcifiers damage antigens and nucleic acids. Record the decalcification method in the LIMS.
